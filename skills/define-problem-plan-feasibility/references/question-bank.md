# Question bank: feasibility plan

## Finding questions
- Which requirements have you never seen met before, or only barely?
- Which requirements pull against each other (speed and cost, safety and ease of use)?
- Which numbers are guesses? Which depend on user behavior nobody has observed?
- Which requirements are flagged TBD? What blocks them?
- What would be most embarrassing to discover late?

## Analyses
- What could we calculate or simulate to see whether this is reachable?
- Which parameters of the surrounding system should we vary (demand, load, environment, cost of inputs)?
- What answer, in what form, would settle it?

## Prototypes
- What do we need to learn: what users do, whether an interface is understood, whether a technology works?
- Throwaway (to learn and discard) or evolutionary (kept and grown)?
- How crude can it be and still teach this? (paper, mockup, subscale, working)
- What are the steps, how long, who does it? Does it depend on another prototype?

## Usability tests
- Who are the real users? Split them by domain knowledge, computer experience and experience with similar systems.
- Which scenarios become the test tasks?
- How will we measure learnability (time to reach a level), efficiency (time for a frequent user), memorability (time for a casual user), errors (by type and severity) and satisfaction (stress, fun)?
- What counts as a pass for each measure?

## Elicitation
- Who might stay quiet in a group? (interviews)
- Where do stakeholders disagree? (facilitated group)
- Where do people do things they cannot describe, especially under stress? (observation)
- Is a questionnaire really the only option?

## Decision criteria
- What result would make you say this requirement is feasible? infeasible?
- At what point would you change the requirement rather than the plan?

## Challenge prompts
- "Could a cheaper analysis answer this instead of a prototype?"
- "Are the people in this test the people who will really use it?"
- "If this comes back infeasible, what do you do?"

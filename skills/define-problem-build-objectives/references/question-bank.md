# Question bank: objectives

## Fundamental objective
- What is the one thing, in aggregate, the stakeholders want from the system in this phase?
- What would they pay more to get more of? What would they pay to avoid?
- Is this the stakeholders' objective, or a way of reaching it?

## Decomposition
- Split that into the parts the stakeholders care about for their own sake (not the ways to achieve them).
- Do any parts overlap or double-count? Is anything important missing?
- Can any part be split further into things you can measure? Stop when each piece is directly measurable.

## Measures
- How would you measure this? In what unit? Higher or lower is better?
- Is it measured outside the system in a defined environment (effectiveness) or as a property of the system (performance)?
- Under which conditions (peak and off-peak, seasons, load, user type)? Is the average enough, or does the tail matter (the worst 5 percent)?
- What is the minimum acceptable level (threshold)? Below this the design is unacceptable.
- What is the level beyond which nobody would notice or pay for more (goal)?

## Weights (swing weighting)
- Imagine every measure at its threshold. Which single improvement, from threshold to goal, would you take first? Give it 100.
- How much is the next improvement worth compared with that? Fill in the rest.
- Would the weights change for another stakeholder group?

## Value curves
- Between threshold and goal, does each step add equal value, less and less value, or more and more?
- Is there a point where value jumps?

## Cost and schedule objectives
- What is the acceptable range of cost per phase? Does the payer in one phase care about cost in another?
- Is there a date after which the value drops sharply?

## Challenge prompts
- "<Node> describes how to achieve something, not what stakeholders value. What do they value that it serves?"
- "The goal and threshold for <measure> are far apart. Is that a real swing, or hedging?"
- "<Stakeholder A> and <Stakeholder B> weigh these differently. Whose value is this?"
